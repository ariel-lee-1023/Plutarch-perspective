# Episodic — the logical residue

**9 texts of 78.** Catalog, reference, and compilation genres that run none of the seven operations
in [`frameworks.md`](frameworks.md).

These are not weak or partial instances of a pillar, and they are not filed here for being minor —
several are among the most-consulted texts in the corpus, and one is the single richest source of
anecdote in ancient literature. They are excluded because they **report rather than reason**: there
is nothing in them that could turn out to have been done wrongly in the sense any of the seven
operations makes available. A cause-hunt can mis-rank; a diagnosis can offer a mark the counterfeit
can fake; a refutation can attack an abandoned formulation. A list of sayings can be inaccurate or
badly sourced, but it cannot be *invalid*, because it advances no inference.

Keeping them separate is what makes the seven categories mean something. Forcing them in would
dilute every pillar that received one — most obviously operation 5, which would absorb three of
these on surface form alone and lose its defining requirement that particulars carry a judgment.

## Verdict boundaries

Three near-misses were reviewed and **kept in the pillars**, because the residue test asks whether
a text runs an operation, not what genre it belongs to:

- **19** *Mulierum virtutes* — a narrative collection, but with a stated general thesis its
  anecdotes serve → `clusters/exemplary.md`.
- **59** *Comparationis Aristophanis et Menandri compendium* — an epitome, but it delivers reasoned
  comparative verdicts that could be wrong → `clusters/exemplary.md`.
- **71** *Epitome De animae procreatione* — an epitome, but it re-runs its parent's decoding in
  compressed form rather than describing it → `clusters/allegorical.md`.

And one in the opposite direction:

- **13** *Septem sapientium convivium* — reads as a wisdom-anthology in narrative dress, and was
  reviewed for residue. Kept in `clusters/dialectical.md` because its sayings are posed as answers
  to problems and set against one another by successive speakers, which is a canvass rather than a
  list.

The operative distinction throughout: **an anthology with an argument is not an anthology; an
argument's raw materials are not an argument.**
