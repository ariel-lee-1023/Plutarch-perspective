# Cluster — Refutative / consistency-testing logic

Operation defined in [`../frameworks.md`](../frameworks.md) §6. **9 texts.**

The governing test: the text fails if the rival can escape by repairing a formulation, or if an
unattractive consequence has been passed off as a contradiction. The most sharply bounded cluster
in the corpus — the anti-Stoic and anti-Epicurean polemics plus two texts that run the same
machinery on non-philosophical targets. The mark of membership is that **nothing is installed in
the rival's place**: the product is a position that cannot be held whole.

| # | Treatise | Vol · offset | The rival, and where the conflict is located |
|---|---|---|---|
| 72 | *De Stoicorum repugnantiis* | XIII · 132820 | The paradigm — self-contradiction is the title and the entire method. Chrysippus is quoted at length and set against himself, doctrine against doctrine and doctrine against his own practice. The §6 quotation rule is enforced rigorously: the contradictions turn on wording, so wording is reproduced rather than paraphrased. Runs the *internal* and *performative* tests side by side. |
| 73 | *Stoicos absurdiora poetis dicere* | XIII · 226875 | A short companion piece: Stoic paradoxes measured against the licence granted to poets, and found to exceed it. The *preanalytic* test in miniature. |
| 74 | *De communibus notitiis adversus Stoicos* | XIII · 231549 | The preanalytic test at full length: Stoic doctrine set against the common conceptions everyone including the Stoic holds before philosophy begins. The complementary half of 72 — where 72 turns the school against itself, this turns it against what it cannot help believing. |
| 75 | *Non posse suaviter vivi secundum Epicurum* | XIV · 156 | Epicurean commitments about pleasure set against what Epicureans must give up to hold them. Dialogue form; the target is the doctrine's internal viability, not its conclusion. |
| 76 | *Adversus Colotem* | XIV · 86959 | Colotes' charge that rival philosophies make life unliveable, turned back by showing his own school cannot survive the test it proposes. A refutation of a refutation, which makes the §6 discipline unusually visible: the argument is entirely about what the opponent is committed to. |
| 77 | *De latenter vivendo* | XIV · 177789 | The Epicurean maxim *live unknown* refuted from the commitments of those who issue it — including the performative point that issuing the maxim publicly violates it. Short, and the cleanest specimen of the performative test in the corpus. |
| 60 | *De Herodoti malignitate* | XI · 149 | The machinery turned on a historian. Herodotus' commitments are located passage by passage and shown to fail *evidentially* — the record does not supply what his account requires, and sometimes supplies against it. The corpus's principal instance of §6's second defect type, kept distinct from internal conflict. |
| 31 | *De virtute morali* | VI · 5908 | Anti-Stoic psychology: the monistic account of the soul set against what the Stoics must say about conflict, akrasia, and the training of the passions. Carries positive Academic-Peripatetic doctrine alongside, and is the most mixed member of this cluster — see the note below. |
| 68 | *De esu carnium* | XII · 283633 | Two declamations dismantling the standing justifications for eating flesh — necessity, nature, custom — rather than arguing a positive dietary doctrine. Protreptic in register, refutative in structure; the weakest fit in the cluster, and flagged as such. |

## Boundary notes

- **The "nothing installed" test** is what separates this cluster from `aetiological.md`. *De facie
  in orbe lunae* (63) demolishes the Stoic account of the moon at length, but it does so to clear
  ground for a cause of its own. These nine texts leave the ground empty.
- **31 is the marginal case in the strong direction.** *De virtute morali* has a positive thesis and
  could be read as doctrinal exposition with polemic attached. It is filed here because the
  argumentative work is done against Stoic commitments and the positive account is largely defined
  by contrast — but a reader tracing Plutarch's own psychology should treat it as doctrine first.
- **68 is the marginal case in the weak direction.** It could be argued into `practical-syllogism.md`
  as protreptic. It stays here because it does not descend to cases or issue rehearsable precepts;
  it attacks the warrants. If the mapping is wrong anywhere, this is the likeliest place.
- **45 is not here.** *De fato* handles rival articulations of fate without setting out to break
  any of them; it lays them out and bounds them. Operation 2, filed in `dialectical.md`.
- **Load together.** 72, 73 and 74 form a single anti-Stoic project; 75, 76 and 77 a single
  anti-Epicurean one. Either trio read alone will understate how systematic the operation is.
