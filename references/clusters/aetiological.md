# Cluster — Aetiological / antiquarian cause-hunting

Operation defined in [`../frameworks.md`](../frameworks.md) §1. **10 texts.**

The governing test: the text fails if it has mis-ranked candidate causes, or has asserted one cause
without canvassing rivals. Every text below takes a datum nobody disputes and opens only its
*why*. Locators are `Vol · char-offset` into `content/PT/Moralia/MORALIA-Volume_<N>.md` in the
Plutarch-Thoughts corpus, marking the title string at which the treatise begins.

| # | Treatise | Vol · offset | The datum, and how the ranking runs |
|---|---|---|---|
| 20 | *Aetia Romana* — Roman Questions | IV · 149 | The purest form of the operation. ~113 Roman customs, each posed as *why do they…?* and answered with candidate causes strung on **"Is it that… Or is it because… Or rather…"**. Verdicts are usually withheld; the ordering *is* the answer. Full repertoire on display — historical, physical, symbolic, etymological — often all four against a single rite. |
| 21 | *Aetia Graeca* — Greek Questions | IV · 115536 | Same engine turned on Greek local custom, names, and obscure civic offices. Shorter, more antiquarian, heavier on the historical and linguistic candidates; the physical candidate nearly drops out where the datum is a title rather than a practice. |
| 62 | *Quaestiones naturales* — Causes of Natural Phenomena | XI · 220581 | The repertoire narrowed to the physical branch. Data are regularities of sea, soil, animals, weather; candidates are material properties. Retains the parallel-canvass form and the refusal to close — the ranking discipline is unchanged, only the candidate pool is restricted. |
| 49 | *Quaestiones convivales* — Table-Talk (9 books) | VIII · 10496 | The largest single instance in the corpus, and the operation put into social form: the *zetema* is posed at table and rival causes are advanced by named diners in sequence. The dialogue frame externalises what elsewhere runs inside one voice — the speakers *are* the candidate causes, and precedence among them is the ranking. |
| 28 | *De Pythiae oraculis* | V · 175113 | Datum: the oracle, once versified, now speaks in prose. Candidates canvassed for the change — decay of the god's power, change in the medium, change in the audience's needs — and ranked toward the last. A cause-hunt where the phenomenon is a *change* in a practice rather than the practice itself. |
| 29 | *De defectu oraculorum* | V · 232189 | Datum: oracles have fallen silent across Greece. The candidate causes are canvassed at length and explicitly weighed — depopulation, the departure or death of daemons, exhaustion of the prophetic exhalation. The clearest case in the corpus of candidates ranked *against each other* rather than merely accumulated. |
| 44 | *De sera numinis vindicta* | VII · 86904 | Datum: the god punishes late. Candidate reasons generated and ranked — the delay as instruction to the observer, as space for repentance, as a debt carried into the family line, as a term of a longer justice. Answers an Epicurean objection, but the objection only opens the *why*; the body of the text is the ranking. Closes with a decoded myth (Thespesius) serving the ranking, per §Combination rule 2. |
| 46 | *De genio Socratis* | VII · 209809 | Datum: Socrates' sign, attested and not in dispute. Candidates canvassed by the company — a sneeze, an omen, a voice, a daemon's touch on the intellect — and ranked toward the last. Embedded in a Theban conspiracy narrative and capped by the Timarchus myth; both are subordinate to the ranking. |
| 63 | *De facie in orbe lunae* | XII · 158 | Datum: the moon shows a face. Rival physical explanations canvassed and weighed — reflection, mixture, an earthy body — with the Stoic account demolished en route. Refutation is heavy here but instrumental: the text would fail by getting the *cause* wrong, not by leaving a rival standing. Sulla's myth closes it as a coda. |
| 64 | *De primo frigido* | XII · 100136 | Datum: cold acts as something positive, not merely as absence. Candidate substrates canvassed — earth, water, air — and ranked, inclining to earth. Closes by inviting the addressee to suspend judgment, which reads as aporia but is the §1 termination condition working correctly: candidates ordered, verdict not forced. |

## Boundary notes

- **49 vs 2.** Table-Talk's questions are frequently disjunctive in surface form, but the engine is
  the accumulation and ranking of *causes*, not the balancing of two limbs. Governing failure:
  a bad cause, not a foreclosed question.
- **63 vs 6.** The anti-Stoic polemic in *De facie* is extensive enough to look governing. It is
  not: the Stoics are cleared away so a cause can be installed. Contrast the texts in
  `refutative.md`, where nothing is installed and the incoherence is the whole product.
- **64 vs 2.** The aporetic close is genuine but does not transfer the text to operation 2 — §1
  terminates on an ordered list, and an ordered list is what it delivers.
- **44, 46, 63 → 4.** All three close with decoded myth. Under §Combination rule 2 the coda serves
  the cause-hunt; see `allegorical.md` for texts where decoding governs instead.
