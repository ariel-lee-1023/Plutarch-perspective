# Cluster — Criterial / differential diagnosis

Operation defined in [`../frameworks.md`](../frameworks.md) §3. **6 texts.**

The governing test: the text fails if its marks cannot be applied before the damage is done, or if
the counterfeit can produce them cheaply. Small cluster, high internal consistency — every member
takes a pair that is confusable *because the imitation has every incentive to pass*, and separates
them by situational signs rather than by definition.

| # | Treatise | Vol · offset | The look-alike pair, and the discriminating marks |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | *Quomodo adulator ab amico internoscatur* | I · 189327 | The paradigm case: friend vs. flatterer. Explicitly framed around the hard problem — the skilled flatterer imitates frankness itself, so frankness cannot be the mark. Marks that survive: constancy of judgment vs. tracking the patron's shifts; blame on matters that cost nothing vs. on matters that cost; conduct when interests diverge; behaviour in the patron's absence. The §3 cheapness criterion is stated and applied throughout. |
| 5 | *Quomodo quis suos in virtute sentiat profectus* | I · 296496 | Real moral progress vs. its appearance — to the agent himself, which is the hardest version, since the counterfeit and the diagnostician are the same person. Signs: dreams, the stability of judgment under interruption, no longer needing an audience, diminishing gap between profession and conduct. Diagnostic throughout; supplies almost no precepts, which is what keeps it out of `practical-syllogism.md`. |
| 7 | *De amicorum multitudine* | II · 27858 | True friendship vs. the crowd of nominal friends. Argues from the criteria of real *philia* — testing over time, single-mindedness, shared adversity — that multiplicity is not a greater quantity of the same thing but a different and lesser thing wearing its name. Pairs naturally with 4. |
| 14 | *De superstitione* | II · 310620 | A three-way discrimination, and the most demanding in the corpus: superstition, atheism, and true piety. Superstition is diagnosed as the worse error precisely *because* it passes for piety while atheism does not — the counterfeit that succeeds is more dangerous than the open denial. Marks drawn from conduct under fear and from what each does in calamity. |

| 42 | *De invidia et odio* | VII · 47228 | Envy vs. hatred. ## The method, stated in the corpus

*De invidia et odio* opens by naming the procedure the whole cluster runs on, and it is the reason
these texts do not work by definition:

> similarities do not so surely make for sameness as dissimilarities make for difference

So the essays never ask *what is flattery?* They ask what the flatterer **cannot** do that the friend
does, and they hunt that difference through a fixed set of situational probes: origin of the
passion, its object, whether it can be avowed, what feeds it, what extinguishes it, and what it
intends. The probes recur across the cluster almost unchanged, which is what makes it the most
internally consistent group in the corpus.

## Three worked diagnoses

**42 *De invidia et odio* — the cleanest small specimen, and the one to read first.** Eight probes
run in sequence against the same pair:

| probe | hate | envy |
|---|---|---|
| **origin** | a judgement that the man is bad, in general or toward oneself | apparent prosperity, and nothing else is required |
| **bounds** | bounded, directed at particular subjects | unbounded — "like sore eyes, disturbed by everything resplendent" |
| **objects** | may take animals — weasels, toads, snakes; Germanicus and the cock | occurs only between man and man |
| **justice** | many are hated justly; hatred of wickedness is praised | no one is ever envied justly, since no one is unjust in being fortunate |
| **avowal** | men confess freely that they hate | no one will say he envies; he calls it anger, and gives it any name but its own |
| **what feeds it** | grows as the hated grows worse | grows as the envied grows **better** — Themistocles as a youth said he had done nothing remarkable, since he was not yet envied |
| **what ends it** | neither the enemy's height nor his ruin ends it | extinguished at both extremes — great fortune blazes it out, misfortune removes its object |
| **intention** | a disposition awaiting the opportunity to injure | absent; the envious would not have their intimates destroyed |

Note what the probes have in common: **every one of them is observable, and none of them requires
access to the man's interior.** That is the §3 cheapness criterion working — a counterfeit can
produce a sentiment; it cannot cheaply produce a pattern of conduct across a man's good and bad
fortune, in your presence and out of it.

**4 *Quomodo adulator* — the hard problem stated, then solved by constancy.** The essay is built on
the admission that the flatterer has taken every ordinary mark: he does kindnesses, he professes the
same loves and hatreds, he composes his nature "like unformed matter" to fit the man he is working
on. He even takes frankness, the mark you would most trust — putting on gravity, "flattering under
the vizard of a frown", dropping in a word of reproof — and that counterfeit freedom is a sham
Hercules with a hollow club, which swells big and sinks under any weight put on it. So frankness
cannot be the mark. What survives is **constancy**: whether the man is "all of one thread, and still
like itself", or changes shape with his company like water taking the figure of the channel it runs
in. The operational test is cheap and repeatable — disparage an acquaintance in his hearing and he
never fancied the man in his life; praise the same acquaintance and he thanks you for the judgement.
Two probes, thirty seconds, no injury required. That is what §3's *applicable in advance* means.

**14 *De superstitione* — a three-way discrimination, and the corpus's costliest verdict.** The two
errors are traced to a single root, ignorance of the gods, running into different soils: atheism in
hardened characters, superstition in tender ones. The discriminating marks are then drawn from
*motion*: the atheist is unmoved regarding the divine; the superstitious man is moved as he ought not
to be. Atheism is falsified reason; superstition is an emotion engendered *from* false reason — and
the added premise, that the gods cause pain and injury, is what makes it the graver error. The
diagnostic pay-off is a behavioural contradiction only the superstitious man exhibits: he fears the
gods and flees to the gods, flatters them and abuses them, prays to them and blames them. The essay
closes on its own warning against over-correction — men fleeing superstition overleap true religion,
which lies between, and land in a hardened atheism. **A diagnosis that leaves the pair no space
between them has failed**, and he says so at the end of his sharpest essay.

## Register — the flattest prose in the corpus

Measured over the six texts: boosters **5.59/1k** against a corpus mean of 3.34 — the plain unhedged
assertion arrives at nearly twice its usual rate, and it arrives *at the marks themselves*. Second
person is **11.6%**, higher than in counsel, because a mark is worthless unless *you* can apply it.
Hedges stay high too (6.64/1k, above the corpus mean): the flat strokes are laid **over** continuing
qualification, never substituted for it.

Named exempla are at their **thinnest** here — about 21 per 1,000 words against 36 in cause-hunting
and 49 in deed-marshalling. Diagnosis is the one operation he runs mostly on types rather than on
named men, because the sign has to generalise to the next flatterer you meet.

Within-volume control (translator held constant): Vol I *criterial* boosters 5.95 vs *practical*
4.22; Vol VII *criterial* 5.46 vs *practical* 4.04 and *aetiological* 3.01. Vol II is flat. Two of
three, which is why the hardening survived into the core.

## Boundary notes

- **The payload test.** Where a text's product is *how to tell apart*, it is here; where the product
  is *how to act*, it is in `practical-syllogism.md`. Applied to two near misses:
  - **43** *De se ipsum citra invidiam laudando* also handles a look-alike (justified self-defence
    vs. offensive self-praise), but its deliverable is a rule for *when and how* to do it. Filed
    under 7.
  - **41** stays here on the same test, in the other direction: the therapy is real but downstream,
    and the essay is titled by the item it discriminates.
- **5 vs 7 (the pedagogical texts).** *De profectibus* is diagnostic where *De audiendis poetis*
  and *De recta ratione audiendi* are instructional, though all three concern a student's
  development. The first asks *have you progressed?*; the others answer *what should you do?*.
- **2 is not here.** *De audiendis poetis* teaches a reader to separate what a poet asserts from
  what he reports, which is discrimination of a kind — but its marks are text-handling rules issued
  to a student, and its failure mode is bad instruction. Filed under 7.
