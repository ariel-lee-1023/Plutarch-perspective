# Cluster — Allegorical / symbolic decoding

Operation defined in [`../frameworks.md`](../frameworks.md) §4. **4 texts.**

The governing test: the text fails if the recovered doctrine cannot stand without the myth, or if
the myth's details are left idle. The smallest cluster, and deliberately so — decoded myth appears
all over the corpus as a *coda*, but in only four texts does the decoding govern. Where it is a
coda it stays with its host (see the boundary notes, and `aetiological.md`).

| # | Treatise | Vol · offset | The encoded surface, and the doctrine recovered |
|---|---|---|---|
| 26 | *De Iside et Osiride* | V · 147 | The paradigm. The Egyptian myth and its attendant rites — dismemberment, the search, the taboos on fish and beans, the shape of the sistrum — read as encoding a metaphysics of two opposed principles with a mediating third. Both §4 constraints are stated and enforced: rival keys are rejected by name (euhemerist decoding, which makes gods into dead kings; physical allegoresis, which makes them into the Nile or the seasons) precisely because they destroy the object they explain. The detail-accountability rule is visible at close grain — the reading is pressed down to individual ritual prohibitions. |
| 27 | *De E apud Delphos* | V · 138904 | A single votive symbol: the letter E dedicated at Delphi. Candidate decodings advanced by successive speakers — numerological, logical, grammatical — and the last given the weight: *EI*, "thou art", as an address to the god's unmixed being. Canvass-shaped like operation 2, but the object is a symbol and the product is a doctrine, which is what governs. |
| 70 | *De animae procreatione in Timaeo* | XIII · 44934 | An authoritative *text* treated as encoding: the soul-mixture, the harmonic ratios, and the temporal creation in the *Timaeus*, decoded into a psychology and a cosmology. Rival interpreters (Xenocrates, Crantor, Posidonius) are refuted en route, but the product is a recovered doctrine, not an exposed incoherence. Detail-accountability at its most literal — the numbers are made to pay their way individually. |
| 71 | *Epitome De animae procreatione* | XIII · 123000 | Plutarch's own compressed restatement of 70, running the same decoding in miniature. Kept in the pillars rather than in `episodic.md` because it *performs* the operation rather than reporting it — see the genre note below. |

## Boundary notes

- **The coda rule (§Combination rule 2) does most of the work here.** *De sera numinis vindicta*
  (44, Thespesius), *De genio Socratis* (46, Timarchus) and *De facie in orbe lunae* (63, Sulla)
  all close with substantial decoded myth and all sit in `aetiological.md`, because in each the
  myth serves a completed ranking of causes. Decoding that arrives after the governing operation
  has finished does not reassign the text.
- **27 vs 2.** *De E* has the outward form of an open canvass — several speakers, several answers.
  It is filed here because the object is a symbol whose surface is inadequate to its reverence, the
  §4 trigger, and because Ammonius' answer is given last and given weight rather than being left
  level with the others.
- **70/71 vs 69.** All three treat Plato as the object. *Platonicae quaestiones* poses problems and
  leaves them open (operation 2); the *De animae procreatione* pair defends a single recovered
  reading. The §4 stretch here is acknowledged: the *Timaeus* is a philosophical text rather than a
  myth or a rite. It qualifies because Plutarch treats its account as deliberately encoded — the
  numbers symbolic, the temporal creation not to be taken at face value — which is the §4 move
  applied to a text that invites it.
- **Genre note on 71 (and on 59 in `exemplary.md`).** Both are epitomes, and epitome is a
  compilation genre. Neither is sent to `episodic.md`, because the residue test in §Combination
  rule 5 asks whether the text *runs* an operation, not what genre it belongs to. Both run one in
  compressed form and could each turn out to have run it wrongly. Contrast the doxography in
  `episodic.md`, which reports what others held and cannot be wrong in that way.
