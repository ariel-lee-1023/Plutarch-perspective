# Cluster — Exemplary / comparative reasoning

Operation defined in [`../frameworks.md`](../frameworks.md) §5. **5 texts.**

The governing test: the text fails if the general judgment is wider than its particulars can carry,
or if the pairing was rigged before it ran. Note the asymmetry this cluster exposes — the corpus is
saturated with *exempla*, but in most texts they are ornament or evidence in someone else's
argument. Only here does the movement from particular to general judgment carry the text.

## The operation stated in its own preface — 19 *Mulierum virtutes*

This is the only text in the corpus where the method is announced before it is used, which makes the
preface the single most important passage in this cluster.

It opens by refusing Thucydides — that the best woman is she about whom there is least talk, censure
or commendation alike, her name shut up indoors like her person — and taking Gorgias instead: not the
form but the **fame** of a woman should be known to many. The Roman practice of a public funeral
oration for women is preferred to both. Then the thesis is stated flat, with no hedge: **man's
virtues and woman's virtues are one and the same.**

The method follows immediately, and it is §5's juxtaposition rule in his own words —

> putting lives beside lives and actions beside actions, like great works of art

with the control specified: does the magnificence of Semiramis have the same character and pattern
as that of Sesostris? He then pre-empts the objection that the pleasure of the anecdotes will do the
persuading, by running the argument first on domains where nobody disputes it. If we exhibited
paintings by women beside those of Apelles or Zeuxis, would anyone say we were aiming at allurement
rather than proof? If we set Sappho beside Anacreon, or the Sibylline oracles beside those of Bacis,
could anyone impugn the demonstration because it delights the hearer? **Establish that delight and
demonstration are compatible in the easy cases, then claim the same licence in the contested one.**
The deeds only begin after that ground is cleared.

That is why this text is here and the apophthegm collections are not: the anecdotes are subordinated
to a thesis stated in advance, and the pairing is specified before a single woman is named.

## 67 *Gryllus* — the comparison rigged against the arguer

The Circe frame is a device, and he tells you so: Circe withdraws from the conversation explicitly so
that no one can say Gryllus is flattering her. What matters is the discipline imposed on the
comparison itself. Gryllus takes the virtues one at a time — justice, wisdom, courage, temperance —
and runs each as a paired case, and before Odysseus can answer the first he is warned off the easy
move:

> see that you don't lose your temper and give me a patriotic answer that isn't what you really
> believe

The question was whether the spontaneously fertile land of the Cyclopes is better than rugged,
goat-pasturing Ithaca, which yields a meagre crop after much toil — and Odysseus concedes: he loves
his own soil more, the other wins his admiration. **The concession is extracted before the analogy
is applied**, which is the §5 requirement that the comparison be capable of going against the
favoured side, built into the dialogue's machinery.

The sharper claim, and the one that carries the cost, is not that beasts have the virtues but that
they have them **untaught** — which is more than most men manage. The dietary consequence follows in
`refutative.md` (68), and the companion debate in `dialectical.md` (66).

## 24 *De Alexandri fortuna aut virtute* — the disjunction as foil

The title promises a canvass and the text does not deliver one, which is precisely why it sits here
rather than beside *De fortuna Romanorum*. Fortune is given a voice only long enough to be answered
by deeds: the wounds taken in front, the philosophy carried into Bactria, the barbarian peoples given
laws and marriages. There is no point at which the essay could turn out for Fortune. Read it against
23 (in `dialectical.md`) and the difference between a canvass and a foil is settled empirically in
one pair of neighbouring texts by one author on one theme.

## 35 *De amore prolis* — the control chosen so the thesis can lose

The general claim is that affection for offspring is natural rather than calculated. The particulars
are drawn almost entirely from animals — and the choice **is** the argument: beasts labour and starve
for their young with no prospect of return, no reputation to gain, and no instruction received.
They are selected because they cannot be suspected of the self-interest the thesis is denying. This
is the cleanest instance in the corpus of particulars picked to control a variable rather than to
illustrate a conclusion, and it is why the text survives §5 despite the material being behaviour
rather than deed.

## Register — the operation in which I nearly disappear

Measured over the five texts: first person **12.3%**, by far the lowest of any argumentative cluster
(cause-hunting runs 26.4%) and barely above the residue's 12.2%. Boosters **1.63/1k**, the lowest
measured anywhere. Sentence length 36.5, the shortest of any argumentative cluster. Named persons run
about **49 per 1,000 words**, the densest outside the collections.

The rule that follows: **marshalling deeds, thin to nothing and let the acts be the subjects of their
own sentences.** No insisting. The deeds are supposed to be doing the work, and a page of them
carrying visible authorial pressure has conceded that they are not.

Second person, by contrast, does not collapse — 8.8%, close to the corpus mean — because the reader
is being asked to grant each deed as it passes. That combination (author absent, reader addressed,
assertion low, names dense) is this cluster's fingerprint.

## Boundary notes

- **The split pair, 23 / 24.** *De fortuna Romanorum* and *De Alexandri fortuna aut virtute* share
  a theme, an antithesis, and a register, and are separated here on operation alone: 23's
  antithesis is never resolved and its structure is the contest itself (filed in `dialectical.md`),
  while 24's antithesis is a foil for a verdict the deeds deliver. Anyone loading one should load
  the other; the pairing is genuine even though the operations differ.
- **67 vs 66.** The companion pieces on animal reason divide the same way: *De sollertia animalium*
  is a judged debate left open (operation 2), *Gryllus* is one-sided and particular-driven
  (operation 5). Load together.
- **19 vs the collections.** *Mulierum virtutes* is narrative and anecdotal, and on genre alone
  would sit beside the apophthegm collections in `episodic.md`. It does not, because of its
  preface: the general judgment is stated, and the anecdotes are subordinated to carrying it. This
  is the sharpest test of §Combination rule 5 in the whole mapping — a collection with an argument
  is not a collection.
- **22 is not here.** *Parallela minora* consists entirely of juxtaposed Greek/Roman pairs, which
  is the §5 form. It is in `episodic.md` anyway, because the pairs support no general judgment; the
  juxtaposition is a filing system rather than an inference. A degenerate instance of this
  operation — the mechanism without the conclusion.
- **35's particulars are not deeds.** §5 speaks of deeds, and animal behaviour is not deed in the
  full sense. It is admitted because the inferential structure is unchanged — particulars chosen to
  control a variable, carrying a general claim — and because the choice of animals *is* the
  comparative control.
