# Cluster — Dialectical problem-posing (aporia)

Operation defined in [`../frameworks.md`](../frameworks.md) §2. **13 texts.**

The governing test: the text fails if it closes a question the evidence cannot close, or leaves
open one it could have settled. Every text below is built on a disjunction whose *canvass* is the
argumentative engine — not merely a disjunction in the title (see §Combination rule 3, and the
boundary notes below, which is where the sorting work in this cluster actually happens).

| # | Treatise | Vol · offset | The disjunction, and how open it is left |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8 | *De fortuna* — On Fortune | II · 45207 | Whether chance or intelligence governs human life. Short, declamatory, both limbs given voice; resolves toward reason without pretending the resolution is a proof. |
| 13 | *Septem sapientium convivium* — Dinner of the Seven Wise Men | II · 232595 | A fictional symposium built as a chain of posed problems — best government, best household, the Egyptian king's riddles — each canvassed by successive sages. Answers accumulate as alternatives, and the seven speakers keep any one of them from becoming the verdict. |
| 23 | *De fortuna Romanorum* | IV · 212016 | Whether Rome rose by fortune or by virtue, staged as a contest between the two as litigants. The most formally open text in the corpus: it breaks off unresolved, and the openness is structural rather than accidental to the argument's design. |
| 25 | *Bellone an pace clariores Athenienses* | IV · 328019 | Whether Athens is more renowned by her generals or her poets and thinkers. Both limbs marshalled at strength; the frame is the comparison itself. |
| 30 | *An virtus doceri possit* | VI · 149 | Whether virtue can be taught. Poses the standing puzzle — we have teachers for every lesser craft — and canvasses it. A theoretical question worked as a question, not a protreptic wearing a question's clothes. |
| 36 | *An vitiositas ad infelicitatem sufficiat* | VI · 235800 | Whether vice alone suffices for unhappiness, or requires fortune's help. Surviving as a fragment; the disjunction is explicit and repeatedly re-posed against itself. |
| 37 | *Animine an corporis affectiones sint peiores* | VI · 243493 | Whether the soul's diseases or the body's are worse. A set thesis worked in schools, and the corpus's cleanest small specimen of the two-limb canvass. |
| 45 | *De fato* | VII · 181390 | What fate is, and how it stands to what is up to us. Expository rather than polemical: rival articulations are laid out and bounded against each other, and the residue is named rather than dissolved. |
| 50 | *Amatorius* — Dialogue on Love | IX · 42237 | Whether the love of boys or of women is the true Eros, staged as a formal debate with advocates on both sides. Inclines to conjugal love, but only after the rival case has been built to its own best form — the §2 requirement met explicitly. |
| 56 | *De unius in republica dominatione* | X · 209816 | Which of monarchy, democracy, oligarchy is best. A three-limb canvass surviving as a fragment; the comparison is of constitutional *types*, which keeps it here rather than in `exemplary.md`. |
| 65 | *Aquane an ignis utilior* | XII · 140521 | Whether fire or water is more useful. A school exercise in pure form — the disjunction is the entire content, and the text is valuable here precisely because nothing else is going on in it. |
| 66 | *De sollertia animalium* | XII · 152953 | Whether land or sea animals are cleverer, with advocates assigned to each side and a judge who declines to rule. Prefaced by an argument that animals reason at all; the disjunction proper is left standing. |
| 69 | *Platonicae quaestiones* | XIII · 153 | Ten exegetical *zetemata* on Plato — why he said this, whether he meant that. Each canvasses solutions and most decline to close. Aporia turned on a text rather than on the world. |

## The machinery: a court, two advocates, and an umpire who need not rule

The cluster's characteristic apparatus is judicial, and it is set up explicitly rather than implied.
In *De sollertia animalium* Optatus is recruited on the page — *am I wrong in supposing that you
will be an impartial and neutral umpire between the young men?* — and seated so that the company can
"return a true verdict on the arguments" without disturbing the tomes of Aristotle. Procedure is
agreed before pleading; the order of the two cases is settled by lot, so that neither side can be
said to have chosen its position. Aristotimus opens with *the court is open for the litigants*.
*Amatorius* uses the same frame: umpires are chosen, and Daphnaeus pleads for one suitor while
Protogenes pleads for the other, each assigned rather than self-selected. *De fortuna Romanorum*
dispenses with human advocates and makes Virtue and Fortune themselves the litigants, "striving for
a decision regarding the hegemony of Rome".

Three things follow from the apparatus, and they are the operation's real content:

1. **Assignment, not conviction.** An advocate argues the side the lot gave him. This is the
   mechanism that makes the §2 requirement — build the weaker case as its partisans would — a
   procedural fact rather than a good intention.
2. **The umpire is empowered and then declines.** Which is the point; see below.
3. **The frame is stated in the first pages**, so a reader cannot mistake the canvass for a
   protreptic wearing a question's clothes. Where a text lacks this apparatus and merely has an
   *An…* title, it is somewhere else (see the boundary notes).

## How the openness is achieved — 66 *De sollertia animalium*

The essay's close is the best single specimen of §2's termination condition in the corpus, and it is
worth having verbatim in shape. Aristotimus finishes: *So, gentlemen of the jury, you may now cast
your votes.* Soclarus does not. He answers with Sophocles on how the strife of rival tongues welds
both sides together, and rules — if it is a ruling — that by combining what the two have said
**against** each other, they will together make a good fight against those who would deprive animals
of reason.

That is not evasion. It is the §2 dissolution clause: the two limbs turn out to be answering a
smaller question than the one that matters, and the residue is named instead of adjudicated. The
prior claim — that beasts reason at all — was established before the debate opened, in the exchange
where the argument runs from sensation to memory to what fears the painful and devises means of
getting what it wants; and the Stoic-Peripatetic objection is given its strongest form on the page
(*if all the beasts partake of reason, justice could not come into existence*) rather than
summarised into weakness.

Notice also how the arguer polices himself. Aristotimus, having run from the dolphins to Odysseus and
Coeranus, breaks off: since he began by promising no tall tales and has now gone past belief, he lays
the penalty on himself of concluding there. **The advocate withdraws his own overreach before the
umpire has to.**

## The range of openness

Not all thirteen texts are open in the same way, and the differences are the cluster's internal
structure:

| kind | texts | what "open" means here |
|---|---|---|
| **Structurally unresolved** | 23, 36, 66, 69 | the text never returns a verdict; 23 breaks off, 66's umpire declines, 69's ten *zetemata* mostly close without closing |
| **Resolved, but only after the rival case is built** | 8, 50 | *De fortuna* inclines to reason, *Amatorius* to conjugal love — each after the other side has had its own advocate at full strength. The §2 requirement met and then exceeded |
| **Dissolved rather than judged** | 66, 45 | the disjunction is shown to be answering different questions, or the rival articulations are bounded against each other and the residue named |
| **Pure school exercise** | 65, 37, 30, 25 | the disjunction *is* the whole content. Valuable here precisely because nothing else is going on: *Aquane an ignis utilior* is the two-limb canvass with every other operation stripped out |

**45 *De fato* is the type specimen of the third row** and the one most often misread. It sets out
rival articulations of fate without attempting to break any of them, bounds them against each other,
and names what is left. Laying positions out and *stopping* is operation 2's success condition; doing
the same thing in `episodic.md`'s doxography is residue. The difference is that here the bounding is
argued.

## Register

Measured over the thirteen texts: questions 3.06/1k and 9.3% of sentences; hedges **5.94/1k**, the
second-highest of any cluster and above cause-hunting; hedge:booster ratio 1.99. First person 22.7%,
second person 7.2% — both close to the corpus mean, because the dialogues distribute the speaking
across named advocates rather than concentrating it in one voice.

The short school pieces run much hotter than the cluster average — *An virtus doceri possit* is 37%
questions, *An vitiositas* 25%, *Aquane an ignis* 16% — while the long judged dialogues run cool
(*De sollertia* 2.8%), because in those the questions have been converted into speakers. **When the
canvass is externalised into persons, the interrogative rate collapses; the disjunction has moved out
of the syntax and into the cast.** That is a modulation rule with no equivalent elsewhere in the
corpus, and it is why a generated "Plutarch dialogue" full of rhetorical questions reads wrong.

## Boundary notes

This cluster's membership is decided almost entirely by §Combination rule 3, since the corpus
contains many disjunctive titles that are not disjunctive arguments.

- **Excluded despite an *An…* title.** *An seni respublica gerenda sit* (54) is a one-sided
  protreptic addressed to a named man and lives in `practical-syllogism.md`. Its disjunction is a
  doorway, not an engine: it would fail by giving the wrong counsel, not by closing a question
  prematurely.
- **24 vs 23.** The *fortune-or-virtue* pair is split. *De fortuna Romanorum* stays here because
  the antithesis is its structure and it never resolves; *De Alexandri fortuna aut virtute* goes to
  `exemplary.md` because its engine is a parade of deeds driving to a settled verdict about one
  man. The pairing is real and worth preserving — see the note in `exemplary.md`.
- **69 vs 70/71.** *Platonicae quaestiones* canvasses readings and leaves them open; the
  *De animae procreatione* group defends one reading against rivals and belongs in
  `allegorical.md`. Same author, same text, different operation.
- **66 vs 67.** *De sollertia animalium* and *Bruta animalia ratione uti* are companion pieces on
  animal reason. The first is a judged debate (here); the second is a one-sided paradox driven by
  particulars, and sits in `exemplary.md`.
- **13 vs 49.** Both are symposia. The *Dinner* canvasses *what is best* — competing answers; the
  *Table-Talk* canvasses *why is it so* — competing causes. That difference sends them to different
  clusters.
